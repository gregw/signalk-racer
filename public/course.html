# Waypoint BTW DTW nm Heading Length nm TWA AWA AWS kn VMG kn ETA Set
Bearings are true. BTW/DTW, shown with the tick box beside Waypoint: bearing and distance from the boat to each point. Leg heading/length: from the previous point of the course. TWA and Set are the wind and current measured on the boat now (or, with Live off, the TWD and TWS, or the set and drift, set above), projected onto every leg as if they hold for the whole course: they are not a forecast (no GRIBs or tidal predictions are used). TWA: a beat within the tack angle, a run beyond the gybe angle (from performance.beatAngle and gybeAngle, or without them the polar's best VMG angles, or 45° and 135°), otherwise a reach; with the favoured tack, green starboard, red port, smaller the closer a beat or run is to even on both tacks. On the point being sailed to, the TWA on the bearing from here is shown beneath. The totals above the table are for the course still to sail, with each beat and run split between tacks. With a polar, AWA/AWS are the apparent wind sailing each leg (on the tack sailed most) at the polar speed with its performance factor, VMG the speed made good along the leg, and ETA the arrival at each point if the wind holds (on a route that is not active, the time from the first point). The current is not allowed for. Set, when the current is known, is an arrow relative to the leg: up with it, down against it, sideways across it, thicker the stronger it is. The next point is highlighted. BTW/DTW published by the server's Course API or a plotter over NMEA are shown below the table. A waypoint moved since the route was made is flagged: the route keeps its own copy of the position, which is what is shown and navigated to.
